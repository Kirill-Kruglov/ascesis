# Derivability

## Status

Foundational analytical document.

This chapter introduces "derivability"
as a provisional research concept.

The objective is not to define a new mathematical quantity.

The objective is to isolate a property that appears repeatedly
throughout the Door-1 investigation,
yet is not completely captured by existing terminology.

Every definition in this chapter is provisional.

---

# Motivation

The Goal Anchor does not require
perfect prediction.

It does not require
perfect compression.

It does not require
perfect control.

Instead,

it appears to require something subtler.

A learner should gradually replace
memorisation
with lawful explanation.

This recurring observation motivates
the concept of derivability.

---

# Observation

During the Justitia programme,
many candidate abstractions were rejected.

The reason was rarely

"low prediction accuracy."

Instead,

they failed because
they did not preserve
the lawful structure
governing future behaviour.

Likewise,

program synthesis,

physics,

formal proof,

and causal reasoning

appear valuable
for the same reason.

They expose rules,
not merely regularities.

---

# Existing Concepts

Several established concepts
capture parts
of the desired property.

Algorithmic compression.

Minimum Description Length.

Kolmogorov complexity.

Predictive State Representations.

Computational Mechanics.

Active Inference.

Causal Models.

Each contributes something important.

None appears,
by itself,
to describe
the complete research objective.

Status

Observation.

Needs literature review.

---

# Working Observation

A learner may achieve
excellent prediction

without discovering
the computational structure
producing those predictions.

Likewise,

a learner may discover
important computational laws

while remaining
imperfect at short-term prediction.

Prediction
and derivation
therefore appear related,
but distinct.

---

# Working Definition 4.1

Derivability

is the extent to which
a computational learner
can replace memorised observations
with lawful computational structure
that continues to support
prediction,
intervention,
and explanation
under previously unseen situations.

Status

Working definition.

Not established.

---

# Important Consequence

Derivability
is not identical to

prediction.

Compression.

Generalisation.

Planning.

Interpretability.

It overlaps
with each of them.

None appears sufficient
on its own.

---

# Law versus Data

Consider two learners.

Learner A

memorises

ten million trajectories.

Learner B

discovers

one hundred executable rules
that generate those trajectories.

Both may predict equally well.

Only one has replaced
observations
with computational structure.

The present programme
is primarily interested
in the second learner.

---

# Interaction Requirement

Derivability
cannot be evaluated
from passive observation alone.

Intervention appears necessary.

Without intervention,

multiple computational hypotheses
may remain observationally equivalent.

Therefore,

interaction
is treated
as a necessary component
of derivability.

Status

Working hypothesis.

Future analytical review required.

---

# Determinism Is Not Required

Suppose
a stochastic environment
is governed
by stable probability laws.

A learner
may derive

transition distributions

instead of

individual trajectories.

The learner still replaces
observations
with computational structure.

Therefore,

lawful stochasticity
appears compatible
with derivability.

This point remains
to be tested.

---

# Derivability versus Memorisation

Memorisation answers

"What happened?"

Derivation answers

"What computational process
produced what happened?"

This distinction
appears central
to the Door-1 objective.

---

# Candidate Properties

A highly derivable environment
might possess
several characteristics.

Compact lawful generator.

Stable intervention effects.

Reusable mechanisms.

Counterfactual consistency.

Compositional structure.

Auditability.

These properties
are hypotheses,
not requirements.

---

# Candidate Failure Modes

Derivability may fail
for several reasons.

The environment
may be

too chaotic,

too weakly observable,

too under-constrained,

too path dependent,

or

dominated
by proxy solutions.

These possibilities
remain open.

---

# Relation to Internal Models

The present programme
does not assume
that every internal model
is highly derivational.

Instead,

it proposes

that environments
capable of inducing
strong internal models

are likely
to exhibit
high derivability.

Whether this implication
is reversible
remains unknown.

---

# Research Questions

Can derivability
be measured?

Can it be approximated?

Can different computational classes
be partially ordered
by derivability?

Does derivability predict
successful world-model emergence?

Does derivability
depend primarily
on the environment,

the learner,

or the interaction?

---

# Candidate Kill-Gates

DG-1

Can a learner
achieve equivalent performance
without discovering
lawful computational structure?

If YES,

derivability
may not be necessary.

---

DG-2

Can highly derivable environments
fail to produce
internal models?

If YES,

derivability
is insufficient.

---

DG-3

Can strong internal models
emerge
inside environments
with very low derivability?

If YES,

the working concept
requires revision.

---

DG-4

Can derivability
be reduced
to an already established quantity
such as

MDL,

algorithmic complexity,

or

predictive state representations?

If YES,

a new concept
is unnecessary.

---

# Summary

Derivability
is introduced
as a research placeholder.

Its purpose
is not
to replace
existing mathematical concepts.

Its purpose
is to isolate
a recurring property
observed throughout
the Door-1 investigation.

The remainder
of the programme
will determine

whether derivability

is

a genuinely useful concept,

a reducible reformulation,

or

an unnecessary abstraction.
