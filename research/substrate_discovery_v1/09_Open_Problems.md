# Open Problems

## Status

Programme-level document.

This chapter concludes the first iteration of the
Substrate Discovery programme.

It intentionally contains questions rather than answers.

The objective of this document is to define the research frontier
after completion of the Justitia, Boundary Analysis,
Faithful Abstraction and Candidate Discovery phases.

Every question listed here should be considered
a candidate research programme.

None is assumed to have a positive answer.

---

# Why End With Questions?

Scientific progress is often measured
by solved problems.

Long research programmes evolve differently.

The most valuable outcome
is frequently the replacement of vague questions
with precise falsifiable ones.

The purpose of this chapter
is to preserve those questions.

---

# Programme Status

The Justitia investigation
did not produce
a successful Door-1 substrate.

However,

it produced

- transferable methodology,

- analytical boundaries,

- faithful-abstraction diagnostics,

- negative knowledge,

- a reproducible research discipline,

- a substantially reduced search space.

The present programme begins
from those results.

---

# Research Frontier

The project no longer asks

"Which environment works?"

The frontier now becomes

"Which computational interaction regimes
make internal-model construction
the rational strategy?"

Everything below
attempts to decompose
this question.

# What Has Been Learned

The Justitia programme
did not identify
the correct substrate.

It identified

what should no longer
be assumed.

Boundary Analysis
did not produce
the desired abstraction.

It identified
where abstractions fail.

Faithful Abstraction
did not establish
a new theory.

It identified
the questions
that any future theory
must answer.

Candidate Discovery
has not identified
the correct substrate.

It has identified
a disciplined procedure
for searching.

The present programme
therefore begins
with substantially
more knowledge
than the project originally possessed.

---

# Closing Observation

Research programmes
rarely fail
because their first hypothesis
is incorrect.

They fail
because they become
attached
to that hypothesis.

The methodology developed
throughout the Justitia investigation
suggests a different philosophy.

Every rejected hypothesis,

every analytical boundary,

every failed abstraction,

and every eliminated substrate class

reduces
the remaining search space.

Progress therefore
should be measured

not only

by successful constructions,

but

by permanent reductions
of uncertainty.

---

# Final Principle

The objective
of Substrate Discovery
is not

to find
a favourite environment.

It is

to identify
the smallest surviving class
of computational interaction regimes

for which

construction
of an internal model

becomes

the rational strategy

for a computational learner.

Whether that class
ultimately exists

remains

the central open question.
