---

# Purpose

PR1 is **not** a new version of the research programme.

It is the first controlled revision of the programme after it has undergone
its own semantic kill-gate review.

The objective is not to improve prose or strengthen arguments.

The objective is to modify the programme itself in a controlled,
traceable and falsifiable manner.

PR1 therefore serves the same role that a software patch serves
for a codebase.

Every accepted modification must have:

- an identified problem;
- supporting evidence;
- explicit behavioural consequences;
- bounded scope.

No silent edits are permitted.

---

# Inputs

PR1 is based exclusively on:

1. Original Substrate Discovery Programme.

# Accepted Programme Changes

## PR1-01

### Problem

Goal Anchor is insufficiently constrained.

Although the programme states that the ultimate objective remains
constructing environments suitable for deriving world knowledge,
the bridge between this goal and the newly introduced Substrate Discovery
programme is not yet demonstrated.

Claude correctly identified that the bridge is asserted
rather than established.

### Evidence

KG0:
Accepted criticism.

### Decision

The Goal Anchor becomes **provisional** until KG1.

### Behavioural consequence

No chapter may treat the current Goal Anchor as established.

Every downstream chapter inherits this provisional status.

---

## PR1-02

### Problem

Derivability has not yet been reduced to existing theory.

The programme currently introduces derivability as a central concept,
without first demonstrating that it is not reducible to existing
frameworks.

### Evidence

KG0:
Accepted criticism.

### Decision

Derivability becomes a gated concept.

KG2 becomes mandatory before further theoretical expansion.

### Behavioural consequence

No new derivability-based claims may be introduced until KG2 is completed.

---

## PR1-03

### Problem

Internal Model lacks an operational definition.

Without an observable criterion,
the central success condition of the programme cannot be measured.

### Evidence

KG0:
Accepted criticism.

### Decision

Internal Model is reclassified as an unresolved construct.

KG3 becomes mandatory.

### Behavioural consequence

Future documents must distinguish between

- intuitive terminology,

and

- measurable operational quantities.

---

## PR1-04

### Problem

The title

"Necessary Properties"

overstates current evidence.

### Evidence

KG0:
Accepted criticism.

### Decision

Necessity becomes a claim requiring explicit justification.

### Behavioural consequence

Every proposed property must declare one of four statuses:

- Necessary
- Desideratum
- Working vocabulary
- Speculative

The burden of proof differs for each category.

---

## PR1-05

### Problem

The emerging Playbook risks becoming procedural ritual.

The existence of templates does not guarantee scientific discipline.

### Evidence

KG0:
Accepted criticism.

### Decision

The Playbook itself becomes subject to future kill-gates.

### Behavioural consequence

Every Playbook rule must eventually demonstrate
that it detects real research failure
rather than merely producing consistent documentation.

---

# New Programme Rules

## PR1-R1

No programme concept may expand
before its corresponding reduction gate has been completed.

---

## PR1-R2

Evidence may only transfer between research stages
through explicit bridge documents.

Implicit transfer of conclusions is prohibited.

---

## PR1-R3

Every newly introduced concept must explicitly identify
existing competing theories.

The programme carries the burden of demonstrating
that the new concept contributes explanatory power.

---

## PR1-R4

Every proposed property must explicitly declare its epistemic status:

- Necessary
- Desideratum
- Working vocabulary
- Speculative

Status may change only through explicit review.

---

## PR1-R5

Review documents do not modify programme documents.

Programme revisions are the only mechanism by which
the programme itself changes.

# Programme Patch Principle

Research programmes evolve through explicit patches,
not silent rewriting.

Every accepted criticism must pass through:

External Review

↓

Programme Review

↓

Programme Revision

↓

Future Verification

before it modifies the programme.

This preserves reproducibility,
maintains historical integrity,
and prevents unnoticed drift of the research agenda.
