# Purpose

The Goal Anchor Identity Gate evaluates whether the current
Substrate Discovery programme is genuinely a continuation
of the original Ascesis objective.

This gate does **not** ask whether the programme is useful.

It asks a much stricter question:

> Is the current programme solving the same scientific problem,
> or has it silently become a different research programme?

---

# Background

Door 1 failed.

The programme therefore shifted from

"constructing a Justitia substrate"

to

"understanding what kinds of computational environments
could support derivable world models."

This transition may represent either

1. a legitimate abstraction,

or

2. an unnoticed change of research objective.

KG1 exists to distinguish these possibilities.

---

# Question

Can the following implication be established?

```
Original Goal

↓

Necessary Intermediate Result

↓

Substrate Discovery Programme
```

rather than

```
Original Goal

↓

Door 1 Failure

↓

Interesting New Research Direction
```

The second path constitutes goal drift.

---

# Hypotheses

## H1

Substrate Discovery is a necessary intermediate step
towards the original objective.

---

## H0

Substrate Discovery is an independent research programme.

The apparent connection to the original objective
is historical rather than logical.

---

# Required Inputs

The analysis must use at least:

- original project memos (v1.1–v1.4);

- Door1 Extracted Knowledge;

- BRIDGE_MAP;

- current Substrate Discovery programme;

- KG0;

- PR1.

No additional assumptions may be introduced
without explicit declaration.

---

# Required Analysis

The following questions must all be answered.

---

## A

Precisely define

the original objective.

Not its implementation.

Its scientific content.

---

## B

List every conceptual transformation between

v1.1

↓

Substrate Discovery.

Each transformation must be classified as

- refinement;

- generalization;

- abstraction;

- replacement;

- unknown.

---

## C

For every transformation,

provide evidence.

No transformation may rely solely
on intuition.

---

## D

Construct the smallest bridge.

The bridge should contain the minimum number
of propositions necessary
to derive Substrate Discovery
from the original objective.

---

## E

Attempt bridge destruction.

Generate the strongest competing explanation.

Example:

Substrate Discovery emerged only because Door 1 failed.

It is therefore a new programme.

---

## F

Attempt bridge reconstruction.

Show why the competing explanation fails,
if it does.

---

# Acceptance Criteria

The bridge survives only if

every conceptual step is justified.

---

# Failure Criteria

The bridge fails if any essential transformation

cannot be justified.

---

# Possible Decisions

## Continue

Goal identity established.

---

## Continue After Revision

Goal identity plausible,

but requires programme modification.

---

## Pause

Insufficient evidence.

---

## Reject

Substrate Discovery is an independent programme.

---

# Deliverables

KG1 must produce

- bridge graph;

- transformation table;

- competing explanation;

- decision;

- consequences for the programme.

---

# Explicit Prohibitions

KG1 may NOT

- redefine the original goal;

- weaken the goal;

- broaden the goal merely to preserve continuity;

- appeal to intuition;

- appeal to future usefulness.

Only logical necessity counts.

---

# Meta Requirement

KG1 should be understandable

without knowledge of Justitia.

If the bridge cannot be explained independently
of the failed substrate,

the bridge is insufficient.

---

# Success Condition

A skeptical reviewer should be able to reconstruct

why Substrate Discovery exists,

using only

the original objective,

Door 1,

and the accepted bridge.

---

# Final Principle

KG1 is passed

only if

the current programme would still have been discovered

by an independent researcher

starting from the original objective

and the verified Door 1 results.

Otherwise,

the programme has drifted.
