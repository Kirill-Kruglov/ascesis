# ASCESIS Research Ontology
## Part I — Foundations & Research Ontology
### Version 2.0 (Post-17F)

---

# 0. Purpose of This Document

This document is not a research paper.

It is not intended to argue that any current hypothesis is correct.

Its purpose is to document the current epistemic state of the ASCESIS project after repeated attempts at falsification.

The document distinguishes four different kinds of statements:

- observations,
- experimentally supported constraints,
- working hypotheses,
- rejected hypotheses.

The primary objective is traceability.

Every important statement should be traceable to one or more experiments, external publications, or explicit logical derivations.

No statement should appear without provenance.

---

# 1. Project Goal

The long-term objective of ASCESIS has remained unchanged since Memo v1.1.

The project does not attempt to construct another statistical language model.

Instead, it asks a more fundamental question:

> Can semantic structure emerge from purely formal generative systems without relying on corpus statistics, embeddings, or externally supplied semantic labels?

Equivalently:

> Is there a minimal mathematical structure from which meaningful descriptions become inevitable?

The project therefore investigates necessary structural conditions for semantic organization rather than empirical prediction.

---

# 2. Research Philosophy

The project follows a falsification-first methodology.

The goal is not to confirm preferred hypotheses.

The goal is to eliminate incorrect explanations.

Every experiment is designed primarily as an attempt to destroy the current working hypothesis.

If the hypothesis survives, confidence increases only slightly.

If it fails, the project immediately adopts the stronger constraint implied by the failure.

This methodology intentionally follows the Popperian view of scientific progress.

---

# 3. Epistemic Discipline

Throughout the project the following principles are maintained.

## 3.1 Separation of observation and interpretation

Every conclusion must explicitly distinguish:

Observed

↓

Measured

↓

Inferred

↓

Hypothesized

These levels are never considered equivalent.

---

## 3.2 Preference for negative results

Negative results reduce hypothesis space.

Therefore they are considered scientifically valuable.

Rejected hypotheses remain part of project knowledge.

---

## 3.3 Constraint-oriented reasoning

The project does not attempt to accumulate positive claims.

Instead it attempts to discover constraints that survive repeated falsification.

These constraints become the foundation for future theory.

---

## 3.4 Substrate independence

No property observed in one substrate is assumed to be universal.

Universality must be tested experimentally.

---

# 4. Confidence Levels

Each statement in the ontology receives one of five confidence levels.

## Level A

Repeatedly supported.

Survived independent attempts at falsification.

Currently treated as an experimental constraint.

---

## Level B

Supported inside the current substrate.

Not yet tested under sufficient substrate variation.

---

## Level C

Working hypothesis.

Useful for generating experiments.

No claim of correctness.

---

## Level D

Speculative idea.

Not yet experimentally investigated.

---

## Level X

Rejected.

Experimentally falsified or substantially weakened.

Rejected hypotheses remain documented.

---

# 5. Evolution of the Central Question

The project has undergone several conceptual shifts.

This evolution is important because many hypotheses were abandoned rather than refined.

---

## Stage 1

Question:

Does a representation-invariant semantic backbone exist?

Working assumption:

Meaning might correspond to globally invariant consequence structure.

---

## Stage 2

After Experiment 17A.2

Question became:

Relative to which perturbations may semantic invariance be defined?

The project abandoned unrestricted invariance.

Representation-preserving transformations became the relevant comparison class.

---

## Stage 3

After Experiment 17C

Question became:

Is consequence invariance sufficient?

Closure-like functional participation became a candidate missing ingredient.

---

## Stage 4

After Experiment 17D

Question became:

Is closure itself a stable observable?

The answer became uncertain.

Closure metrics clustered, but were not uniquely stable.

---

## Stage 5

After Experiment 17E

Question became:

What is the latent geometry of internal observables?

The project temporarily shifted from semantic definitions to latent structure.

---

## Stage 6

After Experiment 17F

Question became:

Which observed latent structures are universal,

and which are substrate-specific?

This is the current state of the project.

---

# 6. Ontology of Knowledge

The project distinguishes five different categories of knowledge.

## Category A

Experimental observations.

These contain no interpretation.

Example:

"Representation-preserving perturbations leave consequence classes unchanged."

---

## Category B

Surviving constraints.

These are observations that have survived repeated attempts at falsification.

Example:

"Semantic equivalence requires an admissible perturbation class."

---

## Category C

Working explanatory hypotheses.

These explain surviving constraints.

They remain provisional.

---

## Category D

Rejected hypotheses.

These remain documented because they delimit future search space.

---

## Category E

Open problems.

Questions that remain unresolved after all completed experiments.
