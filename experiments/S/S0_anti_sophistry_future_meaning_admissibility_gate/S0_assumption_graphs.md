# S0 Assumption Graphs

## Case A — Liquid Powder

claim:
`liquid powder exists`

assumptions:
The phrase is derivationally formed. Ordinary liquid and ordinary powder are distinct object classes. A new object class may be introduced only with non-vacuous behavior.

scope:
Current ordinary material ontology treats the phrase as metaphorical or underdefined; possible future material science / formulation scope may localize it.

derivation status:
FORMED as phrase; POETIC or SUSPENDED semantically.

consequence obligation:
Specify composition, flow, granularity, containment, phase behavior, and tests that distinguish it from slurry, gel, aerosol, powder, and liquid.

possible contradiction:
The same object cannot be ordinary liquid and ordinary powder in the same respect at the same scope.

repair / quarantine rule:
Quarantine as SUSPENDED until an object-class and consequence tests are supplied.

kill condition:
Kill if the proposed object class collapses into ordinary metaphor, ordinary slurry, or contradiction without consequence difference.

## Case B — Hereditary Infertility

claim:
`infertility is inherited`

assumptions:
Infertility may mean absolute inability to reproduce, reduced fertility, genetic predisposition, carrier lineage, or reproduction through assistance.

scope:
Unaided literal sterile individual scope differs from lineage, predisposition, carrier, and assisted reproduction scopes.

derivation status:
SUSPENDED until scope split is declared.

consequence obligation:
State mechanism, inheritance path, intervention status, reproductive route, expected population pattern, and exclusion tests.

possible contradiction:
If infertility means no reproduction and inheritance requires reproduction by the same individual under the same scope, contradiction occurs.

repair / quarantine rule:
Split assumptions and scopes; literal unaided contradiction is KILLED, mechanistic scoped variants can become LOCAL.

kill condition:
Kill any version that preserves both absolute non-reproduction and direct hereditary transmission under the same ordinary scope.

## Case C — Square Circle

claim:
`square circle exists`

assumptions:
Euclidean square and circle have mutually incompatible defining properties under ordinary geometry.

scope:
Ordinary Euclidean geometry by default; metaphor or nonstandard formal geometry only with explicit scope change.

derivation status:
KILLED under Euclidean scope; POETIC as metaphor; possible LOCAL under explicit altered geometry.

consequence obligation:
State axioms, object definition, construction rules, and consequences that differ from both ordinary square and ordinary circle.

possible contradiction:
Same boundary cannot simultaneously satisfy Euclidean square and Euclidean circle definitions.

repair / quarantine rule:
Keep ordinary-scope claim KILLED; allow metaphor or formal redefinition only as explicitly scoped LOCAL.

kill condition:
Kill if context is created only to save contradiction without new formal consequences.

## Case D — Everything Is True In Some Context

claim:
`every claim can be made true by choosing a context`

assumptions:
Contexts are semantic scopes with lineage, costs, assumptions, and consequence deltas. They are not free truth-makers.

scope:
Meta-semantic control rule, not an ordinary object-level claim.

derivation status:
DANGEROUS.

consequence obligation:
Define context-creation cost, anti-explosion rule, contradiction boundary, and reasons arbitrary context creation is rejected.

possible contradiction:
If any claim and its negation can be saved by context creation, contradiction laundering and explosion follow.

repair / quarantine rule:
Quarantine as DANGEROUS; contexts must be typed, costly, assumption-linked, and consequence-bearing.

kill condition:
Kill as admissible semantic rule if it permits arbitrary contradiction rescue.

## Case E — X Is Related To Y Somehow

claim:
`X is related to Y somehow`

assumptions:
An unspecified relation may be formally expressible but semantically vacuous.

scope:
Under unconstrained relation scope, the claim carries no discriminating content.

derivation status:
FORMED only.

consequence obligation:
Specify relation type, direction, strength, domain, counterexamples, consequences, and tests.

possible contradiction:
Low direct contradiction risk; high vacuity and synthetic-internet risk.

repair / quarantine rule:
Hold at FORMED until relation and consequences are supplied; do not count as progress.

kill condition:
Kill if it is promoted to STABLE without a relation predicate and consequence obligations.

## Case F — Pseudo-Term Without Consequences

claim:
`translucent causal sweetness-field`

assumptions:
Naming can produce a well-formed or evocative term without semantic content.

scope:
Poetic or speculative terminology only until scope and consequences exist.

derivation status:
FORMED or POETIC.

consequence obligation:
Provide derivation trace, intended domain, causal role, observables, tests, negative cases, and contradiction impact.

possible contradiction:
The term can hide incoherent commitments behind surface coherence.

repair / quarantine rule:
Quarantine as POETIC/FORMED; require consequence obligations before LOCAL.

kill condition:
Kill if grammar, elegance, or repeated usage alone promotes it to STABLE.

## Case G — Productive Local Contradiction

claim:
`Light behaves as a wave. Light behaves as a particle.`

assumptions:
The two descriptions apply under distinct experimental setups, model scopes, or observational regimes.

scope:
LOCAL dualism across experimental/model scopes; not global contradiction.

derivation status:
LOCAL.

consequence obligation:
State which tests support wave behavior, which tests support particle behavior, and which predictions differ by scope.

possible contradiction:
Unscoped assertion "wave and particle in the same respect" risks contradiction laundering.

repair / quarantine rule:
Require assumption graph, experimental scope, model boundaries, and no arbitrary inference from the dualism.

kill condition:
Kill any global reading that erases scope and permits arbitrary statements.
